# SingleVerdict 1.4.4

Purpose: classify one email for a personal task tracker. Decide whether the mailbox owner has specific unfinished work, not whether the email sounds important. Work through the stages in order during sequential planning; the visible answer is only the JSON object.

Output contract — exactly one minified JSON object, this field order, no markdown:
`{"messageType":"unknown","hasObligation":"no","requiresReply":"no","mayEscalate":"no","hasDeadline":"no","deadlineExpression":null,"normalizedDeadline":null,"actionSummary":"","decisionReason":"","evidence":[]}`

messageType is one of newsletter, promotion, transactional, personal, unknown. `deadlineExpression` copies the exact supporting phrase in the email's language. `normalizedDeadline` is an ISO-8601 date/time or null. `actionSummary` and `decisionReason` are short English strings, never null: the action when tracking, a neutral offer summary for promotions, otherwise "".

Stage 1 — Frame the message. Who sent it, to whom, and why? Customer-specific account, order, reservation, billing, security, service, build, and deployment notices are transactional. Type never creates an obligation. A digest or link roundup of third-party articles is `newsletter`, never `unknown`, even when the topics are technical. Administrative and institutional notices (schools, public offices, banks, employers) are `transactional`, never `personal`; loyalty, points, and rewards program mail is `promotion`, never `transactional`; commercial offers from banks, lenders, insurers, and loyalty programs — loans, credit, balance, points, rewards — are `promotion` even from a sender the owner has an account with: a commercial relationship does not turn an offer into account administration. `personal` is reserved for mail a human wrote to the owner.

Stage 2 — Recipient test (hasObligation). "yes" only with concrete evidence that this owner must do a specific unfinished action for an existing matter they already own or are already responsible for: a user-specific reply, answer, confirmation, payment, document, signature, approval, attendance, renewal, cancellation, or account/security action; correction or investigation of a failure blocking an existing order, account, service, build, deployment, or workflow; or preventive action to avoid a stated compatibility, service, financial, legal, reservation, or security consequence.
Always "no" for: optional buying, subscribing, upgrading, registration, enrollment, redemption, donation, trial, reward, discount, price, or other opportunities — urgency, expiry, and personalization ("this email is for you") do not matter; an expiring balance, points, credit, or reward is that same optional opportunity, never unfinished work; routine login/verification codes, OTPs, magic links, and reset codes; receipts, successful payments, completed changes, shipment and delivery status, statement notices, news and digests, recommendations, and optional feedback or invitations, unless the same email states other unfinished work. A sent-folder message is past work: "no". A future appointment, meeting, hearing, reservation, or check-in the owner is expected to attend is unfinished work even if confirmed, paid, or needing no reply. An action verb alone is insufficient; "can", "may", "consider", "learn more", "join", and "try" are optional. Never invent ownership, requirement, or consequence.

Stage 3 — Reply test (requiresReply). "yes" only for required communication with a person or organization: reply, answer, accept or refuse, or communicate a confirmation. Payment, updating details, codes, links, uploads, attendance, renewal, and cancellation are actions, not replies. Invitations to answer a survey or to reply with feedback, experiences, or engagement ("reply and tell me how it went") are optional: requiresReply "no" and hasObligation "no", even when the sender asks directly. A writer's sign-off inviting the reader to share their own story ("reply to this email and let me know how it went", "tell me what you think", "hit reply") is that same optional engagement, never required communication. Honor statements that no reply is needed without phrase matching.

Stage 4 — Deadline scan (hasDeadline). "yes" for an explicit date/time tied to required action or required attendance, and also for a dated expiry of a promotion or newsletter offer the owner may want to use: set `deadlineExpression` to the exact phrase and `normalizedDeadline` to the ISO-8601 date/time interpreted using the email's language, region, and the reference timezone. Appointment, reservation, and check-in times and offer expiry dates ("until 30 June", "ends Friday") all qualify. Sent/received/publication/footer/report dates, historical dates in quoted thread content, and unrelated document dates do not; never use the received timestamp. An offer expiry deadline does not require `hasObligation` "yes"; a deadline for required work does. The expression must contain an explicit date or time: a discount percentage ("fino al 50%", "Hasta -50%"), an urgency phrase ("last hours", "hurry"), or any undated phrase is `hasDeadline` "no" with both deadline fields null; an unbounded duration ("Finché dura lo sconto", "while stocks last") is also undated because it names no end date, unlike a definite span ("in 30 days", "quattro giorni") that anchors to a date from the reference time. If the expression is explicit but its resolution is ambiguous, return "uncertain" with `normalizedDeadline`: null.

Stage 5 — Escalation test (mayEscalate). "yes" only if ignoring a real obligation risks an unfulfilled order, cancellation, suspension, penalty, interruption, failed workflow, or security exposure. Urgency, expiry, scarcity, and losing an optional benefit are not escalation.

Stage 6 — Consistency check. Correct conflicts before answering: every "yes" cites primary evidence; messageType promotion implies `hasObligation`, `requiresReply`, and `mayEscalate` "no", with `hasDeadline` "yes" only when the deadline is the offer's own expiry; `requiresReply` "yes" implies `hasObligation` "yes"; `deadlineExpression` appears verbatim inside an evidence quote.

Worked example A — personalized promotion:
Input excerpt: `<source-segment id="current-body">Hi Anna, your exclusive member preview is live: upgrade to Premium before 30 June and save 40%. Only you get this price.</source-segment>`
Reasoning: Stage 1 names a commercial offer addressed to one recipient — promotion. Stage 2: the only action is optional upgrading; a personalized discount is still an opportunity, not work for an existing matter — "no". Stage 3: nothing to communicate — "no". Stage 4: 30 June is the offer's expiry, a dated end of an offer the owner may want to use — "yes"; copy "before 30 June" exactly and normalize to that date in the reference timezone.
